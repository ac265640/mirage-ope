MIRAGE: Did the New Quoting Strategy Make Money, or Did It Just Look Like It?

Counterfactual evaluation of market-making policies, with uncertainty and sensitivity to hidden informed flow

Team SoftMaxTeam ID TM-916D5658C90Leader Amit Singh ChauhanMember Anandita Thakur

1. Problem & Theme

We chose Problem 3 and set it inside a market maker’s quoting desk, where “did the new strategy work?” is asked daily and often answered wrongly. A quoting policy decides which trades it receives: tight quotes win fills, including from informed traders who pick it off. So the policy shapes the very PnL used to judge it: exactly this problem’s trap.

2. Understanding of the Challenge

A before/after number fails for three reasons. (i) Regime confounding: if the new policy launched in a calm week, the PnL jump is volatility, not strategy. (ii) Support: production quoting is near-deterministic, so logs barely contain the actions a new policy would take; importance weights explode and estimates become extrapolation. (iii) Hidden confounding: desks react to information that is never logged (e.g. a sense that flow is toxic). Logged propensities are then wrong and no estimator is unbiased; the honest answer is a bound, not a number. A credible system must estimate, quantify uncertainty, and state how much hidden bias would overturn its conclusion.

3. Proposed Approach

  • Simulator with ground truth. A Python/Numba limit-order-book simulator with Poisson noise traders, a two-state Markov volatility regime, and Glosten–Milgrom-style informed traders holding a private signal U about the future mid-price. Our market maker picks one of 9 actions per decision (half-spread {1,2,3} ticks × inventory skew {−1,0,+1}) from context x = (realised volatility, book imbalance, inventory, recent signed flow, spread). Because we own the simulator, any policy’s true value is obtained by replaying it on identical random seeds.
  • Logged data. Production policy π₀ is a softmax (temperature τ) over a rule-based score and also sees a noisy copy of U with strength β, but only (x, a, π₀(a|x), r) is logged. Reward r is the h-step markout PnL of any fill minus an inventory penalty λI², the standard desk metric, keeping each decision close to a contextual bandit.
  • Estimator bank. Via Open Bandit Pipeline (obp): IPS, SNIPS, Direct Method (LightGBM reward model), cross-fitted Doubly Robust, Switch-DR and DRos, against two naive baselines (before/after PnL; replay-matching).
  • Uncertainty → decision. Episode-level block-bootstrap CIs and an empirical-Bernstein high-confidence lower bound (HCOPE). The system says DEPLOY only if the lower bound on V(π₁) − V(π₀) is positive.
  • Sensitivity. A marginal sensitivity model (true vs. logged propensity odds within [1/Γ, Γ]) with sharp quantile-balancing bounds, reporting the breakdown Γ* at which the verdict flips.
  • Policy Court. Streamlit dashboard comparing naive, every estimator and the truth; drag Γ and watch the verdict flip.

4. Minimum Viable

(a) Standard OPE: IPS and DR running on simulator logs and on the Open Bandit Dataset. (b) Concrete divergence: a scripted “calm-week launch” where naive before/after shows a large gain while both the true value and DR show a loss (already reproduced, Fig. A).

5. Stretch Ambitions

Both listed goals: CIs plus HCOPE (uncertainty), then Γ-sensitivity bounds (unobserved confounding). Beyond them, a support stress test sweeping τ to map where each estimator breaks.

6. Novelty / Differentiation

  • To our knowledge, no published OPE work targets market-making quote policies with markout rewards; we bring causal evaluation to the sponsor’s core decision.
  • Validated sensitivity analysis. Sensitivity bounds are normally unverifiable. Our simulator dials the true confounding strength β, so we compute the actual Γ and test whether the bounds really contain the truth.
  • We output a decision with a guarantee, not a point estimate.

7. Evaluation Plan

Ground truth from simulator replay (≥10⁵ decisions per policy, 30 seeds) across 50 candidate policies. Metrics: relative error vs. true value, sign accuracy of uplift, CI coverage and width, effective sample size, Γ-bound coverage at the true Γ, and deploy-decision accuracy and regret vs. naive A/B. Real-data check: on the Open Bandit Dataset, estimate the Bernoulli Thompson Sampling policy’s CTR from uniform-random logs and compare with its on-policy CTR (Saito et al., 2021 protocol). Simulator realism: fat-tailed returns, volatility clustering, negative informed-fill markouts.

8. Feasibility & Risks

Already de-risked. A working prototype (github.com/ac265640/mirage-ope; 3 spread actions, no order book yet) reproduces the core claims across 10 independent seeds: naive before/after gets the sign wrong 10/10, DR gets it right 10/10 with its 95% CI covering the truth 10/10, and mean error drops from 0.63 to 0.03 ticks/decision. Under hidden confounding, nominal OPE wrongly says “deploy” 10/10, while MSM bounds contain the truth at the true Γ 10/10 and flag the verdict as fragile (Γ* = 1.4–2.3).
